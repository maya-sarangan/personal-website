My friends and I sat on the lawn, discussing what we were going to do at our next sleepover. We planned out the perfect schedule. We would eat dinner, pizza of course, then we would set up our sleeping bags. We would watch movies, play for some time,....... but then what? We didn't have any idea on what to do during that time. 

One of my friends suggested playing truth or dare, but the idea was shot down, since we have already played it too many times. My other friend suggested playing skeletons in the closet, but we had already shared all of our secrets, we had nothing else. Then, I suggested just sharing cool facts that we know. My friends all agreed, and we decided to have a trial run, to have a better idea on how it would go during the sleepover. 

One of my friends explained how to find the difference between a Labubu and a Lafufu. You have to check the number of teeth they have. A real Labubu will have nine teeth, but a Lafufu will usually have more. My next friend told us that sharks are actually older than trees! Sharks came around 400 million years ago, but trees only made an appearance around 350 million years ago. Then, it was my turn. I asked them if two different numbers could be equal, and they said no. I mean, how can two different numbers be equal? That is what today's post is about, how can two different numbers be equal? Let's begin!

First, let's think of a variable. We can use x.

```
x = 0.99999.......
```

Now, multiply 10 to both sides of the equation. You will get:

```
10x = 9.9999......
```

Now, subtract the two equations. The result will be:

```
9x = 9
x = 1
```


But how? We already established that x = 0.999999 at the beginning, how can x = 1? Does this mean 0.99999..... equals 1? Let's take a look at another example to better understand why this happens.

```
1/3 = 0.3333......
```

Now, let's multiply both sides by three

```
1 = 0.999999
```

How could this happen? Let me explain. If we mark 0.99 on a number line, we can clearly see that it is less than 1. There are still many numbers between 0.99 and 1. Even 0.999 is less than 1, and so is 0.9999. 

But here's the strange part: the dots in 0.999... mean that the 9s continue forever. There isn't a final 9 where we can say, "Okay, now we're done." The gap between 0.999... and 1 isn't some tiny number. There is no gap at all.

So 0.999... and 1 are actually two different ways of writing the same number.

An easier way to think about it is that since 1/3 = 0.333...., why can't 1 = 0.999. Since 1/3 is a fraction it can be easier for our brain to process the fact that they are equal. But since 1 is a whole number, and 0.999... looks like a decimal that is just a little less than 1, we think that it has to be less than one, and that they will never be equal.

So I guess my friends were right. Two different numbers can't be equal. But two different ways of writing the same number can be. My friends and I decided to use this game for the sleepover! But, we ended up falling asleep before we could do it.... Oh well. At least we discovered that 0.999... = 1 before we fell asleep.
