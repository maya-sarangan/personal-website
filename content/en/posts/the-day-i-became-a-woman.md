What does freedom mean?

Does it mean being allowed to play outside? Being allowed to ride a bicycle? Being allowed to spend your own money? [The Day I Became a Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Day_I_Became_a_Woman) explores these questions through three stories about women at different ages.......

It is a windy day and Hava's mom is waking Hava up from her bed inside of a small white - cloth tent. Hava is a small, energetic girl with black, wavy hair up to her shoulders with short bangs. She is wearing sandals and a frock that hangs up to her knees. She lives with her grandmother and mother. They are a Muslim family living in Iran, and in her tradition, a girl becomes a woman on her ninth birthday. Once Hava gets out of bed, she is informed by her grandmother that it is her birthday. Therefore, Hava has now become a woman. She must wear a [Chador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chador) and she cannot talk or play with boys. Hava's grandmother and mother immediately start sewing Hava a Chador to wear.

![Hava](/personal-website/images/Hava.jpg)

This upsets Hava since it means that she can no longer play with her best friend, Hassan. She pleads with her grandmother and argues that she still has one hour until her actual time of birth, 12:00. Her grandmother allows Hava enjoy her last hour of being a child. She hands Hava a stick, saying that when the stick's shadow disappears, Hava should be home. Hava immediately goes to Hassan's house and asks him to come outside, but Hassan's sister says that Hassan is working on homework. So, Hava tries to talk to Hassan through the bars of his window. Hassan says that he cannot come outside until he is finished, but he hands Hava some money and asks her to go to the market and buy sweets and ice cream. Once Hava returns with the goods, they start enjoying it together. Hava even shares her lollipop with Hassan. Hava asks Hassan if he can come outside now, but Hassan persists with a stern no since he hasn't finished his schoolwork. Hava complains that time is running out, but Hassan's answer remains the same.

Hava now walks over near the beach and sits in the sand. She sees two older boys tying barrels together with rope. She asks them what they are doing, and they say that they are building a raft to sail on. They ask Hava if she would like to come. Hava says no. They say that it will only be a quick trip, but she says that she must stay here. So the boys leave and Hava is left alone. The stick's shadow disappears shortly, and Hava's mom comes to take Hava home. And the viewers move on to the next story...

It is a sweltering hot summer day and a biking race is taking place. The participants are all girls and one of them is Ahoo. She is dressed in a flowing black chador covering everything except her face. She is fighting to get first place when she suddenly hears her name being shouted. Her husband is on a horse and is beside her, yelling at her to come home. He says that she must cook and clean and that women must stay at home. He says that she should not disobey her own husband and that she is committing a sin. Ahoo ignores him and keeps biking. Her husband threatens to call the imam, but Ahoo still doesn't care. The imam arrives and speaks in soft tones to Ahoo. He says that her dear husband is faithfully waiting for her at home and that she must take care of him. He begs Ahoo to stop but she keeps going. Ahoo's husband divorces her on the spot.

![Ahoo](/personal-website/images/Ahoo.jpg)

Soon her grandfather, followed by the other village elders, comes in horses and rides alongside Ahoo, begging her to come home. All of them are dressed in simple white linen pants and nothing on top. Her grandfather berates her, telling her that she is not being a good person and that she should come home at once. Ahoo ignores him and carries on. Another elder steps up and tells Ahoo to at least listen to him. He also begs Ahoo to come home, but Ahoo, once again, ignores him. Soon the elders get angry and warn Ahoo to beware of her brothers. The elders leave, and Ahoo is far ahead in the race, alone. Her two brothers block her path on horses. They charge at her and break her bike, forcing her to come home. The viewers are left wondering about the fate of Ahoo....

Next comes the story of Hoora. A group of boys are talking amongst themselves waiting for a plane to arrive near the airport. Once they spot a plane coming, they immediately rush over to help the passengers with their bags. An old woman in a wheelchair comes out. She is wearing a chador. A boy comes over to help push her wheelchair. The boy asks the old lady where she would like to go. She says she wants to buy household appliances, so he brought her to a mall. On the way there, she tells that boy that she has a horrible memory and that she tied ribbons to her fingers as reminders on what to buy. She even says that she is irritated because she forgot to leave food for her rooster back at home. At the mall, she gapes at the women wearing t - shirts and jeans and she looks at all the modern makeup in awe. She buys a refrigerator, decorated plant pots, a water jug, etc. Once she buys all of her goods, she asks the boys to lay them out somewhere so she can see all of it. The boys decide to take it to the beach so it can be shipped off and taken with her back home. On the way to the beach, the woman tells the boy pushing by a wheelchair that she doesn't remember what the last ribbon on her finger was for and that no matter how much she tries to remember it, it just won't come.

![hoora](/personal-website/images/hoora.jpg)

She tells the boy that she should have married her first love instead of her husband and even asks the boy if he wanted to be her son. The boy declines saying that he already has a mother and father. Once they lay everything out at the beach, the old lady goes back to the shop, saying she needs to check if she can remember her forgotten item. While she is gone, the boys start playing with everything she bought, even her wedding dress. Even after that she still couldn't remember the last item. Soon, the boys start loading all her items on a raft so it can be taken to the ship. The old lady asks a boy to stay behind and make tea for her. She complains that the tea kettle is clear and naked, but the boy says that is how the kettle is meant to be. She asks the boy if he wants to be her son. She tells him that she has all of these items to take home with her. The boy declines and says that he already has a Mother.After this, two biker girls from Ahoo's story come. The old lady invites them to join her for tea. Once they are settled, the biker girls explain Ahoo's story. The old lady then tells them about her forgotten item and they look around at everything she bought. They come to the conclusion that she has already bought all household items and that she is not missing anything there. The old lady tells them to leave, even though they haven't had any tea yet and she boards one of the rafts. As she drifts away the two biker girls ask her name. The old lady says her name is Hoora. Even Hava from the first story is here, holding her mother's hand, and seeing Hoora drift away towards the ship.

The movie "The Day I became a Woman" was 2000 Iranian Film directed by Marzieh Meshkini. In Hava's story, we can see that the moment she becomes a woman she is stripped of all her privileges, showing how difficult it is to be a girl child. The innocent child who shamelessly shares the lollipop with Hassan is forced to severe ties with her best friend in the name of the religion and local customs. 

In Ahoo's story, it isn't actually about biking. It is about Ahoo trying to gain her freedom, while overcoming obstacles, like people trying to stop her. It is a sweltering hot day, yet Ahoo is still expected to wear the full body chador. And the elders are wearing loose pants, and no tops. They do not follow their religious rule for dressing. Yet, they expect the women to follow the unfair rules commanded to them. This is the injustice Ahoo had to face. Even Ahoo's husband says that it is Ahoo's job to cook and clean. And, have you wondered why only Ahoo is being chased and not the other participants? It is because Ahoo is the only married person, so her family and husband force her to come home and fulfill her duty as a loyal wife.

Finally, let's look at Hoora's story. Have you wondered why she is buying all of this, at her age? Even if it is not shown directly, it is implied that her finances were controlled by her husband and that he never permitted her to buy these things. We can assume that he passed away and that she is coming here to buy the things she could never have her whole life. And the sad part here is that she was only able to do this when she was old and in a wheelchair. 

Try to hazard a guess on what the last ribbon could have been. There are many answers, such as freedom, a loving family, etc. I think the last ribbon was for a son. Since she went back to the store and checked, but nothing, this implies that the item cannot be bought. And the two girls said it couldn't have been a household item. And, the old lady kept asking the boys if they wanted to be her son. She even bragged to one of them that she has all of these items, almost like the whole purpose of buying them was for a son, to love him and to be loved.
 
I loved the ending scene. Here, all the girls from the three different parts are shown in one scene. There are many different ways we could interpret this. First, one could see this as the three phases these people have to go through. Hava, who is still a child, the two biker girls are still trying to get married, while Ahoo is trying to escape and be free. Hoora is trying to experience everything she couldn't in her past years since she was married. Another way to interpret this is, the three phases of a woman. Not three different people, but the phases of life experienced by one.

This beautiful movie was directed by Iranian woman [Marzieh Meshkini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marzieh_Meshkini). She was born in 1969 in Tehran, Iran. She went to the University of Tehran before going to study cinema for eight years at Makhmalbaf Film School. Other movies by her are, "Stray dogs", "The Man Who Came With The Snow", "Buddha Collapsed Out of Shame", "The President", and "Marghe and Her Mother". This film "The day I became a Woman" won the UNESCO Award, the Isvema Award, and the Silver Hugo for Best First Film award. 

The three stories take place at different ages of life, but they all ask the same question: who gets to decide how a woman should live? For me, the movie's answer is that every person deserves the freedom to make their own choices.
