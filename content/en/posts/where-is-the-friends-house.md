["Where Is the Friend's House?"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where_Is_the_Friend%27s_House%3F) is a 1987 Iranian movie by [Abbas Kiarostami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_Kiarostami).

![Where_Is_The_Friends_House_1](/personal-website/images/WhereIsTheFriendsHouse-1.jpg)

A group of boys are having fun and playing during class. Their teacher is a few minutes late, and they take full advantage of this, disrupting other classes and joking around. Once their teacher finally comes in, he scolds them profusely and tells them that they should study next time. He comes around collecting homework and stops at Mohammad Reza Nematzadeh's desk. Mohammad fumbles for his homework, but the teacher is most displeased when he realizes that Mohammad did his homework on loose sheets of paper instead of his notebook. The teacher chides Mohammad, saying that if he did this one more time, he would be expelled. Mohammad cries and promises to always use his notebook for homework. His friend, Ahmad, sits beside him, trying to comfort him.

Once Ahmad gets home, he is greeted by his mother tending to his baby brother, drying the clothes, and urging Ahmad to finish his homework and go get bread. Ahmad agrees and sits down to do his homework, occasionally pushing his baby brother's crib to help him sleep. But when he opened his satchel, he found out that he had accidentally taken his friend Mohammad's homework instead. He urges his mom to let him go and give it back. But his mother, preoccupied by the baby, says no and tells him to go do his homework. She points at Ali, and says, "Look! Ali has finished his homework and is going out to play. Go do the same!". Ahmad protests and begs his mother to let him go. His mother asks where Mohammad lives, and Ahmad responds by saying that he lives in Poshteh. His mother puts her foot down and says no, Poshteh is too far. So, Ahmad decides to sneak away to find Mohammad, and he starts running over the hills to Poshteh. The elders watch as he goes, wondering why Ahmad is leaving to Poshteh.

Once he reaches Poshteh, he stumbles upon a group of old bartering men. They are discussing the price of doors and who can make it cheaper. One of them just got a deal, and grabs Mohammad's notebook out of Ahmad's hands and writes in it, saying that Mohammad won't notice. Ahmad follows one of them, and realizes, that he is actually the father of one of his classmates. He asks his classmate where Mohammad lives and he doesn't know, but he does give Ahmad the instructions for his cousin's house. Ahmad ventures through Poshteh to find it, he finally thinks he found it, but it turns out that his classmate had given him the wrong instructions.

Dejected, he starts walking home but he is greeted by a friendly carpenter who offers to take him instead. The carpenter tells Ahmad that he knows the Nematzadeh family and could have taken him from the start. On the way there, the carpenter complained to Ahmad, saying that too many people are switching to using iron doors, and that his wooden doors were better. Once they finally reach there, Ahmad realizes that this is also wrong. It turns out that there are many Nematzadeh families in Poshteh and nobody knew exactly who Ahmad was talking about. Ahmad runs back home as the sun goes down and is met by his grandfather and the other elders.

Ahmad's grandfather tells Ahmad to go get a pack of cigarettes, but Ahmad protests saying he has to buy bread and get home. But, he soon reluctantly agrees and runs off to go get some. The elder next to his grandpa says, "Hey, I've got some cigarettes right here, you can have some.". The grandpa responds by explaining that he already had a pack of cigarettes, it was that children should always do what they are told. He explains that he had to tell Ahmad twice before he went to go get some cigarettes, his father would have only had to tell him once. When he was a boy his father would give him some allowance and beat him every week, he would sometimes forget the allowance, but he would never forget the beating. He would always find some excuse to beat him. 

Ahmad returned with the cigarettes and ran off. He gets home and refuses to eat, upset that he wasn't able to give his friend his notebook back. He tells his mom that he is going to do his homework and he leaves. The next day, the teacher walks around collecting people's homework, Ahmad arrives just in time with Mohammad's notebook. It turns out that he had done Mohammad's homework in addition to his own. The teacher comes around, stopping at both of their desks to check their homework. He takes his pen, and in one swift motion, gives them both a check. But tucked inside the pages of the homework, there is a small pink flower.....

The core of this movie is the innocence of Ahmad and his ethical clarity. He didn't care how far away the house was, all he cared about was giving his friend his notebook back.

Ahmad begs his mother to let him go to his friend's house. His mother represents the adult world, where everyday responsibilities and household problems can become so overwhelming that adults sometimes fail to understand what matters to children. Even though Ahmad is acting out of good reason, she still doesn't understand.

Once Ahmad reaches the bartering men, they completely disregard what Ahmad has to say, they just take the notebook and damage it. These men represent the adults' world, not caring about what the children have to say. To them, the children are essentially voiceless. This scene can also be interpreted as the Present and the Future. The men, the Present, are unknowingly spoiling the future by grabbing the notebook from Ahmad, the Future.

Now, look at the old carpenter complaining about people switching to iron doors. He represents the past, and their reluctance to change and try something new. He just wants things to stay the same way that they were back then. In other words, he can't let go of the past.

When Ahmad's grandfather makes a comment on how children must be obedient, even if what they are being told to do is nonsense, this represents the past trying to pass on their problems and issues to the future. The grandfather did not care that Ahmad had to get home, all he could think about was his opinions, and his problems, which he was forcing Ahmad to fix. We can even see that when the grandfather was just a child, his father would beat him for no reason every single week in the name of obedience. Now, the grandfather expects the future generations to act the same way, showing once again that the past is not willing to accept change.

Finally, at the ending scene with the flower, it may seem insignificant, but really, the flower represents something that the past — the carpenter — hands down to the future — Ahmad. It is the kindness that must be preserved even after all the opposition of the adult world. Ahmad rebelled, and didn't do what he was told, showing that he was ready to break out of the line of obedience so that he could do a good deed. The flower shows us that change will happen slowly, one small act of rebellion at a time...
